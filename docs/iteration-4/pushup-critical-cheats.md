# Push-up critical cheats — Iteration 4 S1

Status: agreed scope for implementation (2026-11-12); thresholds and measurements remain under test.

## Critical invalid movements

Per the user's decision, these must remain invalid even during fast repetitions:

1. **Incomplete arm extension / lockout:** an attempted repetition without returning to the required extended-arm top position. Preserve the existing `incompleteExtension` meaning and distinguish momentary landmark loss from actual evidence of bent arms.
2. **Knees on the floor:** knee-supported push-ups are invalid (`kneesDown`). Front-view hips/knees can be occluded or inaccurate; absence of reliable evidence is not proof either way.
3. **Belly on the floor:** supporting or resting the belly on the floor is invalid (`bellyDown`) when the available observations provide reliable evidence.

Do not remove existing head-nod, shallow-depth, upright-body or other checks merely because they are not listed above. Hold duration and timing may be tuned independently, with measured changes to both valid counts and incorrect counts. A change must not silently turn any previously rejected critical-cheat fixture into a counted repetition.

## Outstanding validation

Fast full repetitions, incomplete lockout, knees, belly contact, low-confidence/missing landmarks and varying camera angles require replay and real-device checks. Current labels and monocular occlusion do not establish perfect detection; state missed cases and regressions explicitly.

## Isolated parameter experiments

Measured against the same in-sample frozen data and historical fixtures with
`tools/diagnose/s1_parameter_sweep_test.dart`. Baseline M1/M2/M3: 50/63,
188/207, 124/207. Reducing smoothing tau from 65 ms to 45 ms produced
51/63, 188/207, 127/207 respectively and did not increase the number of
counted knee/belly events in the historical fixtures. However, one previously
`kneesDown` event became `armsStraight`; the existing exact replay test failed.
This candidate was not applied. Reducing the lockout threshold to 170 degrees
produced 50/63, 186/207, 123/207 and newly counted two incorrect events in one
frozen case; it was also not applied. These counts do not establish safety
against previously unseen camera placements.

A synthetic 30 Hz, one-second full repetition was added to the judge tests and
passes using the original production smoothing. The focused judge, metric,
historical replay and frozen-evaluation test run passed 121 tests; nine known
error tests were skipped. An independent video-based labelled rep evaluation
and a change that improves game-speed counting without regressing critical
cheats are still required; S1 is not complete.

## Exploratory video-only training

`tools/diagnose/s1_reference_video_experiment.py` sampled eight frames per
Mendeley clip, extracted MediaPipe Lite landmarks, and trained a random forest
on provider good/bad clip labels. Subjects 001–018 were training and 019–024
were held out across all views. Five of 144 clips had insufficient detected
poses. All views: 104 training clips, 35 test clips, 15/35 correct (42.9%).
Front only: 32 training clips, 11 test clips, 6/11 correct (54.5%). The held-out
majority-class baseline is 18/35 overall and 6/11 front-only, respectively.
Thus these results do not demonstrate useful generalisation. The held-out
set includes the two contradictory label pairs noted above; clip counts are
small and the numbers must not be read as app or per-rep accuracy. The
experiment does not detect lockout, knees, belly, repetition count, or S2
identity. MediaPipe is not the app's ML Kit detector. This exploratory model
was not integrated. Results, including individual predictions, are stored at
`C:/SWPP/iter4/pushup-reference-videos/s1-reference-experiment.json`.

## Limited game-speed readiness change (2026-11-15)

The default top-pose hold is reduced from 300 to 250 ms for initial
arming. Clearing a prior knee latch retains a 300 ms minimum, including when
hips are hidden. This is not a hold required after every repetition. The 172-degree less-extended-arm lockout remains unchanged: an
isolated reduction to 171 degrees newly counted one labelled incorrect rep
on frozen case `20261031-220617_s26-fe_normal` (170 degrees counted two).
The result does not support safely relaxing that angle.

With only the hold changed, frozen M1/M2/M3 stayed 50/63, 188/207 and
124/207, respectively. The event verdict sequences of every frozen and
historical case were identical in a direct before/after replay, including the
historical knee and belly cases. Reproduce from `gymrats_app/` with
`flutter test --no-pub ../tools/diagnose/s1_game_speed_sweep_test.dart` and
`flutter test --no-pub ../tools/diagnose/s1_verdict_compare_test.dart`.
These are in-sample landmark replays, not an independent accuracy evaluation.
The frozen set already includes incorrect counts; unchanged does not mean all
cheats are detected. Synthetic 30 Hz tests cover a one-second correct rep,
knee and belly reps, and a fast incomplete lockout. Real-device UAT and a
human-verified video evaluation are still required. This limited change does
not claim the broader S1 accuracy goals are complete.

A separate exploratory belly boundary change from -0.08 to -0.12 shoulder
widths improved frozen M2/M3 from 188/207 and 124/207 to 191/207 and 127/207
with M1 unchanged, but changed two previously `kneesDown` historical verdicts
to `bellyDown`. It was not applied; these reason regressions require a safer
design. Diagnostic output is from `s1_belly_sweep_test.dart` and
`s1_belly_compare_test.dart`. An independent reviewer also found that reducing
the shared hold unmodified would clear a knee latch too early with hidden hips;
the release minimum and its regression test address that specific case.
